RES. 1581IN THE HOUSE OF REPRESENTATIVESSeptember 24, 2026Mr. McDowell submitted the following resolution; which was referred to the Committee on Energy and CommerceRESOLUTIONSupporting the goals and ideals of Red Ribbon Week during the period of October 23 through October 31, 2026. 
Whereas the National Family Partnership started the Red Ribbon Campaign in 1988 to— (1)educate the people of the United States about the link between drug use and violence; 
(2)preserve the memory of Enrique Kiki Camarena (referred to in this preamble as Special Agent Camarena), a special agent of the Drug Enforcement Administration who— (A)served the Drug Enforcement Administration for 11 years; and 
(B)was murdered in the line of duty in 1985, while engaged in the battle against illicit drugs; (3)commemorate the service of Special Agent Camarena to the Drug Enforcement Administration and the people of the United States; and 
(4)further the cause for which Special Agent Camarena gave his life; Whereas the Red Ribbon Campaign is the most longstanding drug use prevention program in the United States, bringing drug awareness to millions of people in the United States each year; 
Whereas Red Ribbon Week is celebrated every year during the period of October 23 through October 31 by— (1)State Governors and attorneys general; 
(2)the National Family Partnership; (3)parent-teacher associations; 

                (4)
 Boys and Girls Clubs of America;  (5)the Young Marines; 
(6)the Drug Enforcement Administration; and (7)hundreds of other organizations throughout the United States; 
Whereas the objective of Red Ribbon Week is to promote the creation of drug-free communities through drug use prevention efforts, education programs, parental involvement, and community-wide support through specific actions such as lighting up buildings and landmarks, and activities that engage the public; Whereas the Drug Enforcement Administration’s Fentanyl Free America campaign supports the goals of Red Ribbon Week by educating the public about the dangers of illicit fentanyl, promoting drug-free and healthy lifestyles, preventing overdoses, and encouraging communities to work together to save lives; 
Whereas, according to the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, drug overdose is the leading cause of death in people in the United States between the ages of 18 to 45, outnumbering deaths by firearms, motor vehicle crashes, suicide, or homicide; Whereas approximately 70,000 people died from drug overdoses in the United States in 2025; 
Whereas reducing the demand for illicit controlled substances would— (1)curtail lethal addictions and overdoses; and 
(2)reduce the violence associated with drug trafficking; Whereas, although public awareness of illicit drug use is increasing, emerging drug threats and growing epidemics continue to demand attention; 
Whereas the Drug Enforcement Administration launched an upgraded initiative called Every Day is Take Back Day wherein locations of year-round drop boxes are identified for the public to safely dispose of unused or expired prescription drugs that can lead to accidental poisoning, overdose, or misuse; Whereas synthetic opioids, such as fentanyl and the analogues of fentanyl, devastated communities and families at an unprecedented rate, claiming more than 38,000 lives in 2025; 
Whereas the Drug Enforcement Administration’s Special Operations Division is essential to the Nation’s efforts to combat illicit drugs because its intelligence-sharing and deconfliction capabilities help law enforcement partners coordinate safely and effectively, disrupt trafficking networks, and prevent deaths caused by fentanyl and other dangerous substances; Whereas the presence of fentanyl, the analogues of fentanyl, and other synthetic opioids (i.e. nitazenes, orphines, etc.) pose hazards to police officers and law enforcement agents; 
Whereas 29 percent of pills tested at Drug Enforcement Administration laboratories contain a potentially deadly dose of fentanyl; Whereas the Drug Enforcement Administration has created a special exhibit and website titled The Faces of Fentanyl Memorial Wall and Kiosk to commemorate the lives lost from fentanyl poisoning, and has received over 7,500 photos as of the date of enactment of this resolution; 
Whereas the Drug Enforcement Administration seized more than 47,000,000 fentanyl-laced, counterfeit prescription pills and nearly 10,000 pounds of fentanyl powder in 2025; Whereas the Drug Enforcement Administration’s laboratories continue to identify xylazine, a veterinary drug used to sedate animals with no legitimate use in humans, in fentanyl powders and pills making the fentanyl even deadlier; 
Whereas the Drug Enforcement Administration’s laboratories are identifying other dangerous and deadly synthetic drugs, to include nitazenes and orphines, in the drug supply chain; Whereas, according to the Substance Abuse and Mental Health Services Administration, among people aged 12 or older in 2025, 69,800,000 people used illicit drugs in the past year; 
Whereas, according to the Substance Abuse and Mental Health Services Administration, the percentage of people aged 12 or older who misused prescription opioids in the past year decreased from 3 percent, or 8,500,000 people, in 2021, to 2.4 percent, or 6,900,000 people, in 2025; Whereas, in 2026, from January 1 to June 29, 2026, the Drug Enforcement Administration seized over 120,000 pounds of methamphetamine and an estimated nearly 3,000,000 methamphetamine-laced counterfeit prescription pills, which is over 7 times the number of pills seized in 2020; 
Whereas drug overdose deaths involving psychostimulants, such as methamphetamine, rose from 5,777 in 2015, to over 26,000 deaths in 2025, and now outpaces overdoses involving cocaine; Whereas the number of cocaine overdose deaths in 2025 was nearly 3 times the number in 2015; 
Whereas nearly 20,000 United States citizens died in 2025 from an overdose involving cocaine; Whereas parents, young people, schools, businesses, law enforcement agencies, religious institutions and faith-based organizations, service organizations, senior citizens, medical and military personnel, sports teams, and individuals throughout the United States will demonstrate their commitment to healthy, productive, and drug-free lifestyles by wearing and displaying red ribbons during the week-long celebration of Red Ribbon Week; and 
Whereas the National Family Partnership is forming a partnership with the grassroots group Fentanyl Fathers to stream the movie Dead on Arrival nationally to educate and inspire the public to participate in solving the fentanyl epidemic: Now, therefore, be it That the House of Representatives— 
(1)supports the goals and ideals of Red Ribbon Week; (2)encourages children, teens, and other individuals to choose to live drug-free lives; and 
(3)encourages the people of the United States to— (A)wear and display red ribbons during Red Ribbon Week to symbolize their commitment to healthy, drug-free lifestyles; 
(B)light up buildings and landmarks to send a drug-free message; (C)promote the creation of drug-free communities; and 
(D)participate in drug use prevention activities to show support for healthy, productive, and drug-free lifestyles.
