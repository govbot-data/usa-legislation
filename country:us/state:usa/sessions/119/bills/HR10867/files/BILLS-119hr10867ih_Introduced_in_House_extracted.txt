R. 10867IN THE HOUSE OF REPRESENTATIVESOctober 22, 2026Mr. Frost (for himself and Ms. Lee of Florida) introduced the following bill; which was referred to the Committee on Ways and Means, and in addition to the Committee on Natural Resources, for a period to be subsequently determined by the Speaker, in each case for consideration of such provisions as fall within the jurisdiction of the committee concernedA BILLTo prohibit the importation of certain species of sloth, and for other purposes. 
1.Short titleThis Act may be cited as the Stopping Legalized Organized Trafficking and Harm to Sloths Act of 2026 or the SLOTHS Act of 2026. 2.Prohibition on importation of certain species of sloth (a)In generalExcept as provided in subsection (b), it is unlawful for any person to import any individual of a covered sloth species.  
(b)ExceptionA person may import an individual of a covered sloth species, in whole or in part, if— (1) (A)the individual or part thereof is dead; and 
(B)such importation is carried out for research purposes, as demonstrated by the person through documentation to the satisfaction of the Secretary; or   (2) (A)such importation is carried out— 
(i)to move the individual to a covered institution; and (ii)in accordance with a breeding program of a covered institution; and  
(B)the person demonstrates through documentation to the satisfaction of the Secretary that the individual was not taken in the wild after the date of the enactment of this section.   (c)Penalties (1)Civil penalties (A)In generalExcept as provided in subparagraph (B), any person that violates this section may be assessed a civil penalty by the Secretary of not more than $20,000 for each such violation. 
(B)NoticeNo civil penalty may be assessed under subparagraph (A) unless the person accused of the violation is given notice and opportunity for a hearing with respect to the violation. (C)Separate offensesEach violation shall be a separate offense and the offense shall be deemed to have been committed not only in the district where the violation first occurred, but also in any district in which a person may have taken or been in possession of the said sloth. 
(2)Criminal penalties 
(A)In generalWhoever knowingly violates this section shall be fined not more than $100,000.  (B)Separate offensesEach violation shall be a separate offense and the offense shall be deemed to have been committed not only in the district where the violation first occurred, but also in any district in which the defendant may have taken or been in possession of a sloth.  
(3)Cancellation of Federal authorizationsIf a person is found to have violated this section, the Secretary shall cancel any Federal license, permit, or other authorization issued to the person by the Secretary that authorizes the person— (A)to import or export fish or wildlife or plants; or 
(B)to operate a quarantine station or rescue center for imported fish or wildlife or plants.  (4)Secretary not liable for certain paymentsThe Secretary is not liable for the payment of any compensation, reimbursement, or damages in connection with the cancellation of any license, permit, or other authorization pursuant to this section.  
(d)Forfeiture 
(1)In generalAny individual of a covered sloth species imported contrary to the provisions of this section or any regulation issued pursuant thereto, shall be subject to forfeiture to the United States.  (2)Application of customs laws (A)In generalAll provisions of law relating to the seizure, forfeiture, and condemnation of property for violation of the customs laws, the disposition of such property or the proceeds from the sale thereof, and the remission or mitigation of such forfeiture, shall apply to the seizures and forfeitures incurred, or alleged to have been incurred, under the provisions of this section, insofar as such provisions of law are applicable and not inconsistent with the provisions of this section. 
(B)Exercise of powers under customs lawsAll powers, rights, and duties conferred or imposed by the customs laws upon any officer or employee of the Treasury Department may, for the purposes of this section, also be exercised or performed by the Secretary or by such persons as the Secretary may designate. (C)Issuance of warrantsAny warrant for search or seizure issued pursuant to this section shall be issued in accordance with rule 41 of the Federal Rules of Criminal Procedure. 
(3)Storage costAny person convicted of an offense, or assessed a civil penalty, under subsection (c) shall be liable for the costs incurred in the storage, care, and maintenance of any individual of a covered sloth species seized in connection with the violation concerned. (4)Civil forfeituresCivil forfeitures under this section shall be governed by the provisions of chapter 46 of title 18, United States Code.  
(e)DefinitionsIn this section: (1)Covered institutionThe term covered institution means an institution, zoo, aquarium, or other facility accredited by the Association of Zoos and Aquariums.  
(2)Covered sloth speciesThe term covered sloth species means a species of the suborder Folivora. (3)Fish or wildlifeThe term fish or wildlife has the meaning given the term in section 2 of the Lacey Act Amendments of 1981 (16 U.S.C. 3371). 
(4)ImportThe term import has the meaning given the term in section 2 of the Lacey Act Amendments of 1981 (16 U.S.C. 3371). (5)PersonThe term person includes any individual, partnership, firm, joint stock company, corporation, association, trust, estate, or other legal entity. 
(6)PlantThe term plant has the meaning given the term in section 2 of the Lacey Act Amendments of 1981 (16 U.S.C. 3371).  (7)SecretaryThe term Secretary means the Secretary of the Interior.
