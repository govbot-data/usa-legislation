R. 10807IN THE HOUSE OF REPRESENTATIVESOctober 13, 2026Ms. Tlaib (for herself, Ms. Garcia of Texas, Ms. Lee of Pennsylvania, Ms. Omar, Mrs. Ramirez, Ms. Velázquez, and Mrs. Watson Coleman) introduced the following bill; which was referred to the Committee on Oversight and Government Reform, and in addition to the Committee on the Judiciary, for a period to be subsequently determined by the Speaker, in each case for consideration of such provisions as fall within the jurisdiction of the committee concernedA BILLTo prohibit the use of automated mass surveillance systems and associated components, and for other purposes. 
1.Short titleThis Act may be cited as the Get the Flock Out of Our Communities Act. 2.Prohibition on use of automated mass surveillance systems (a)Prohibition on use of Federal fundsNotwithstanding any other provision of law, no Federal funds made available before, on, or after the date of the enactment of this Act for any fiscal year or otherwise made available to any agency, State, or unit of local government may be obligated, expended, reprogrammed, or transferred to purchase, deploy, install, maintain, access, lease, or operate any automated mass surveillance system, including any of the following: 
(1)Automatic license plate reader or associated component. (2)Biometric surveillance system. 
(3)Facial recognition technology. (b)Prohibition on use of automated mass surveillance systemsAn agency, or employee or officer of an agency acting in an official capacity may not acquire, possess, access, solicit, or use in the United States— 
(1)any automated mass surveillance system; or (2)information derived from an automated mass surveillance system. 
(c)RemovalEach agency, State, or unit of local government that purchases, deploys, installs, maintains, leases or operates or is in the process of purchasing, deploying, installing, maintaining, leasing, or operating any automated mass surveillance system using Federal funds shall remove or order a lessee to remove any such automated mass surveillance system not later than 30 days after the date of the enactment of this Act. (d)Disposal of dataAn agency, State, or unit of local government or employee or officer of an agency, State, or unit of local government acting in an official capacity that possesses automated mass surveillance data prohibited under subsections (a) or (b) shall permanently delete such data not later than 7 days after the date of the enactment of this Act. 
(e)InadmissibilityAny data obtained in violation of this section may not be admitted into evidence in any court or administrative proceeding. (f)Eligibility for certain Federal grants (1)Certification required for certain grantsAn application by a State or a unit of local government for funding under a covered program shall include a certification that such State, unit of local government, and any law enforcement agency to which the State or unit of local government will distribute funds, does not maintain, access, lease, or operate any automated mass surveillance system or information derived from an automated mass surveillance system. 
(2)Eligibility for certain grantsA State or unit of local government shall be ineligible for funding under a covered program without providing the certification described under subsection (b). (g)DefinitionsIn this section: 
(1)AgencyThe term agency has the meaning given that term in section 551 of title 5, United States Code. (2)Associated componentThe term associated component, with respect to an automated mass surveillance system, includes any product, service, hardware, software, algorithm, database, communications network, cloud-based service, or data analysis platform of, or used in connection with, such system to collect, store, process, analyze, compare, query, match, transmit, share, or retrieve data collected or generated by such system, including biometric data. 
(3)Automated mass surveillance systemThe term automated mass surveillance system means any automated system or technology that acquires, processes, collects, analyzes, generates, retains, uses, or stores information or data on an indefinite or large number of people. (4)Automatic license plate readerThe term automatic license plate reader means any camera or camera system product or service that automatically captures license plate data of vehicles, and any associated information. 
(5)Biometric surveillance systemThe term biometric surveillance system means any computer software that performs facial recognition or other remote biometric recognition in real time or on a recording or photograph that allows or confirms the unique identification or verification of an individual and is generated from the measurement or processing of unique biological, physical, or physiological characteristics, including any of the following: (A)Fingerprint. 
(B)Voice print. (C)Iris or retina imagery scan. 
(D)Facial or hand mapping, geometry, or template. (E)Deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA). 
(F)Gait. (6)Byrne grant programThe term Byrne grant program means any grant program under subpart 1 of part E of title I of the Omnibus Crime Control and Safe Streets Act of 1968 (34 U.S.C. 10151 et seq.), without regard to whether the funds are characterized as being made available under the Edward Byrne Memorial State and Local Law Enforcement Assistance Programs, the Local Government Law Enforcement Block Grants Program, the Edward Byrne Memorial Justice Assistance Grant Program, or otherwise. 
(7)Cops grant programThe term COPS grant program means the grant program authorized under section 1701 of title I of the Omnibus Crime Control and Safe Streets Act of 1968 (34 U.S.C. 10381). (8)Covered programThe term covered program means any program or activity funded in whole or in part with funds made available under— 
(A)a Byrne grant program; and (B)the COPS grant program. 
(9)Facial recognition technologyThe term facial recognition technology means technology that facilitates or otherwise enables an automated or semi-automated process that assists in identifying an individual based on the physical characteristics of the face of the individual, or that logs characteristics of the face, head, or body of an individual to infer emotion, associations, activities, or the location of an individual. (10)StateThe term State means each of the several States, the District of Columbia, and each commonwealth, territory, or possession of the United States.
