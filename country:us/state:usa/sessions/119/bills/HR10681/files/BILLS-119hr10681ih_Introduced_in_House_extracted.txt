I119th CONGRESS2d SessionH. R. 10681IN THE HOUSE OF REPRESENTATIVESOctober 1, 2026Ms. Lee of Nevada (for herself and Mr. Fulcher) introduced the following bill; which was referred to the Committee on Natural ResourcesA BILLTo establish a National Critical Minerals Council within the Executive Office of the President to develop and coordinate the implementation of a national critical mineral and material strategy for the United States, and for other purposes. 
1.Short titleThis Act may be cited as the National Critical Minerals Council Act. 2.National Critical Minerals CouncilSection 7002 of the Energy Act of 2020 (30 U.S.C. 1606) is amended— 
(1)by redesignating subsection (o) as subsection (p); and (2)by inserting after subsection (n) the following: 
 
(o)National Critical Minerals Council 
(1)DefinitionsIn this subsection: (A)AdvisorThe term Advisor means the Chief Minerals Advisor designated under paragraph (4)(C). 
(B)Advisory CouncilThe term Advisory Council means the National Critical Minerals Council established under paragraph (3). (C)Co-ChairThe term Co-Chair means an individual listed under paragraph (4)(A). 
(D)Critical mineral or material projectThe term critical mineral or material project means any project that, as a primary function or purpose, identifies, characterizes, mines, extracts, processes, reprocesses, reuses, or recycles critical minerals or critical materials. (E)Institution of higher educationThe term institution of higher education has the meaning given the term in section 101 of the Higher Education Act of 1965 (20 U.S.C. 1001). 
(F)Mining schoolThe term mining school means— (i)a mining, metallurgical, geological, or mineral engineering program accredited by the Accreditation Board for Engineering and Technology, Inc., that is located at an institution of higher education, including a Tribal College or University (as defined in section 316(b) of the Higher Education Act of 1965 (20 U.S.C. 1059c(b))); or 
(ii)a geology or engineering program or department that is located at a 4-year public institution of higher education located in a State the gross domestic product of which in 2021 was not less than $2,000,000,000 in the combined categories of “Mining (except oil and gas)” and “Support activities for mining”, according to the Bureau of Economic Analysis. (G)Relevant congressional committeesThe term relevant congressional committees means— 
(i)the Committees on Energy and Natural Resources, Armed Services, and Foreign Relations of the Senate; and  (ii)the Committees on Natural Resources, Energy and Commerce, Armed Services, and Foreign Affairs of the House of Representatives. 
(2)PurposesThe purposes of the Advisory Council are— (A)to develop and coordinate the implementation of a national critical mineral and critical material strategy for the United States to secure the critical minerals and critical materials needed for energy security, economic opportunity, and national defense purposes; 
(B)to coordinate with relevant critical mineral and critical material efforts across relevant Federal agencies; (C)to reduce reliance on foreign adversaries for critical mineral and critical material imports; 
(D)to support domestic mineral sourcing capacity; and (E)to reduce single points of failure in critical mineral and critical material supply chains of the United States. 
(3)EstablishmentThere is established in the Executive Office of the President a National Critical Minerals Council, to advise the Co-Chairs and Advisor. (4)Staff (A)Co-ChairsThe Advisory Council shall be co-chaired by— 
(i)the Secretary (or a designee); (ii)the National Security Advisor (or a designee); and 
(iii)the Director of the National Economic Council (or a designee). (B)Advisor (i)In generalNot later than 180 days after the date of enactment of this subsection, the Co-Chairs shall designate a Chief Minerals Advisor. 
(ii)DutiesThe Advisor shall— (I)carry out the day-to-day operations of the Advisory Council; 
(II)have hiring authority for Advisory Council staff and serve as the principal staff director for activities of the Advisory Council within the Executive Office of the President; (III)serve as the executive secretary of the Advisory Council; and 
(IV)report to the Co-Chairs. (iii)PositionThe Advisor shall be— 
(I)a Senior Executive Service position (as defined in section 3132(a) of title 5, United States Code) in the Department of the Interior; or  (II)an equivalent level official within the Executive Office of the President. 
(C)MembershipThe Advisory Council shall comprise such heads of Executive agencies (as defined in section 105 of title 5, United States Code) or their appropriate designees, including at least 1 representative from each of— (i)the Department of Agriculture; 
(ii)the Department of Commerce; (iii)the Department of Defense; 
(iv)the Department of Energy; (v)the Department of the Interior; 
(vi)the Department of Labor; (vii)the Department of State; 
(viii)the Department of the Treasury; (ix)the Committee on Foreign Investment in the United States; 
(x)the Development Finance Cooperation; (xi)the Environmental Protection Agency; 
(xii)the Export-Import Bank of the United States; (xiii)the National Economic Council; 
(xiv)the National Security Council; (xv)the Office of Management and Budget; 
(xvi)the Office of Science and Technology Policy; (xvii)the Office of the Director of National Intelligence; and 
(xviii)the Office of the United States Trade Representative. (5)DutiesIn carrying out this subsection, the Advisory Council shall— 
(A)coordinate the development of interagency policy guidance related to critical minerals and critical materials, including guidance issued jointly through the processes of the National Security Council and the National Economic Council; (B)recommend alignment of Federal agency budget requests with the national critical minerals and critical materials strategy and provide advice to the Director of the Office of Management and Budget on that alignment; 
(C)identify duplicative or conflicting Federal agency policies and recommend harmonization across represented Federal agencies; (D)coordinate with the heads of all relevant Federal agencies to consistently and proactively track investments in critical mineral or material projects and mining-related projects around the world made by the People's Republic of China and other foreign entities of concern, as determined by the Advisor; 
(E)take actions to improve the visibility of the United States as a preferred partner for critical mineral or material projects and agreements in comparison to foreign adversaries, including collaborating with non-United States entities and governments from allied and partner nations; (F)track and advise on memoranda of understanding or agreements with allied and partner nations for the exchange of resources, including financing, workforce, and innovation; 
(G)facilitate the availability, development, and environmentally responsible production of domestic resources to meet national critical minerals and critical materials needs; (H)seek to ensure there is an evaluation and consideration of the impact to current and future supplies of critical minerals and critical materials in proposed Federal agency decisionmaking with respect to proposed decisions that have the potential for significant positive or negative impacts to the supply of those critical minerals and critical materials; 
(I)consult with outside experts from mining schools and other institutions of higher education, National Laboratories (as defined in section 2 of the Energy Policy Act of 2005 (42 U.S.C. 15801)), relevant private industries, relevant nongovernmental entities, Indian Tribes, and investors to stay apprised of current commercial practices, industry trends, research trends, industry needs, and best practices for responsible critical minerals and critical materials sourcing; (J)coordinate mining and critical mineral or material project research, development, deployment, investment, and commercialization funding priorities and opportunities across all relevant Federal agencies;  
(K)develop a central platform on which funding opportunities may be viewed by private entities; (L)maintain a central, digitized dashboard accessible to all agency staff, developers, and the public, to serve as a comprehensive resource for tracking projects and housing data across Federal agencies, encouraging transparency and coordination; and 
(M)coordinate mining, metallurgical, geological, and critical mineral education and workforce development efforts to enable implementation of the national critical minerals and critical materials strategy. (6)ReportNot later than 1 year after the date of enactment of this subsection, the Advisor, in consultation with the Co-Chairs, shall submit to the relevant congressional committees a report on— 
(A)the composition and function of the Advisory Council; (B)a national critical mineral and critical material strategy to achieve the purposes described in paragraph (2); and 
(C)an implementation plan that— (i)describes how represented agencies intend to carry out that national critical mineral and critical material strategy; and 
(ii)includes clear benchmarks to evaluate how the strategy and implementation plan are fulfilling the purposes described in paragraph (2). (7)BriefingNot later than 1 year after the date the report required under paragraph (6) is submitted, and annually thereafter, the Advisor shall provide to the relevant congressional committees a briefing on the progress of the Advisory Council in executing the implementation plan and updates to the national critical mineral and critical material strategy described in that report..
