        RESOLUTION
        Recognizing the 179th anniversary of the arrival of pioneers
            belonging to The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints to the Great Salt Lake
            Valley, honoring the enduring pioneer spirit that shaped the American West, and
            celebrating its continued relevance to the unfolding American story.
    
    
        
 Whereas the early members of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints (referred to in this preamble as the Church) sought nothing more than the freedom to worship according to the dictates of their own conscience;
        
        
 Whereas, in the years following the establishment of the Church, the early members of the Church endured repeated persecution, including mob violence, the destruction of their homes and crops, the loss of their property, and forced displacement from one settlement after another;
        
        
 Whereas, on June 27, 1844, Joseph Smith, the leader and prophet of the Church, was murdered by an armed mob, leaving the Church and its members facing an uncertain future;
        
        
 Whereas Brigham Young, who succeeded Joseph Smith as leader of the Church, concluded that safety and religious liberty could only be found far beyond the settled United States and organized the Church's members for a journey into the wilderness of the American West;
        
        
 Whereas, beginning in February 1846, thousands of Latter-day Saint pioneers set out across the frozen Iowa plains and ultimately traveled more than 1,300 miles by wagon and, for many, by foot, crossing prairies, deserts, mountains, and rivers to reach their new home;
        
        
 Whereas the Latter-day Saint pioneers endured extreme cold, disease, hunger, and exhaustion during the journey, and many families buried children, parents, and spouses along the trail, their graves marking a path of sacrifice across the American West;
        
        
 Whereas, upon entering the Great Salt Lake Valley on July 24, 1847, Brigham Young declared, This is the right place, foretelling how the valley would become home to many Latter-day Saint pioneers and their posterity;
        
        
 Whereas, within days of their arrival, the Latter-day Saint pioneers laid out a city, plowed fields, irrigated the desert, and began to build homes, mills, and churches where none had stood before;
        
        
 Whereas, over the following decades, tens of thousands more Latter-day Saint pioneers followed the same trail, and, together with earlier arrivals, established more than 500 communities throughout the American West, transforming the region into a place of prosperity;
        
        
 Whereas the industry of the Latter-day Saint pioneers extended far beyond their own settlements, as they built roads and bridges, operated ferries across major rivers, planted way-station crops, and assisted thousands of other westward-bound emigrants who relied on the resources and goodwill the Latter-day Saint pioneers had established;
        
        
 Whereas, on July 24, 1849, the Latter-day Saint pioneers held their first formal commemoration of the anniversary of their arrival, gathering for a procession and a public celebration of thanksgiving in Salt Lake City;
        
        
 Whereas that observance has continued for 177 years and is now celebrated annually as Pioneer Day, a Utah State holiday marked by parades, rodeos, reenactments, fireworks, and civic gatherings that welcome participants of every faith and background;
        
        
 Whereas the story of the Latter-day Saint pioneers is, at its core, a distinctly American story of a people who, having been denied protection and refuge, built both for themselves and for the generations who followed;
        
        
 Whereas the pioneer spirit of self-reliance, enterprise, and perseverance that carried the Latter-day Saint pioneers across the plains continues today in the character of the American West, which remains a place where individuals and communities join together to solve hard problems and build for the future; and
        
        
 Whereas the legacy of the Latter-day Saint pioneers stands as a lasting reminder that the American story is written not only by the founding generation, but by every generation that chooses to press forward with unconquerable faith and undaunted hope: Now, therefore, be it
        
    
    
        
 That the Senate—  (1) recognizes the 179th anniversary of the arrival of Latter-day Saint pioneers to the Great Salt Lake Valley on July 24, 1847, and the annual observance of Pioneer Day that commemorates it;
            
            
                (2)
 honors the extraordinary sacrifice of the Latter-day Saint pioneers, including those who gave their lives along the trail, in pursuit of religious liberty and a place to call home;
            
            
                (3)
 commends the Latter-day Saint pioneers and their descendants for their foundational role in settling and developing the American West, and for a legacy of industry, cooperation, and faith that continues to benefit the United States; and
            
            
                (4)
 encourages all the people of the United States to reflect on the enduring virtues of the Latter-day Saint pioneers and to carry forward, in their own generation, that same spirit of resilience in the continuation of the American story.
